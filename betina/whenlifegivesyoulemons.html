A Creative Solution

One morning, a shop owner arrived at work and discovered that someone had broken his shop window. There was a large hole in the glass.

Instead of simply covering the damaged area with a piece of cardboard, he decided to do something unusual. He used a boot to cover the hole in the window. The result was creative and attracted people's attention.

Some people thought his solution was funny and positive. Others pointed out that the solution wasn't very practical and that problems like this happened too often in the area.

The shop owner couldn't change what had happened, but he found a creative way to respond to it.

Exercise 2 – Writing Challenge: When Life Gives You Lemons
Read the situation and write 100–130 words.
You have been preparing an important presentation for several days. On the morning of the presentation, your computer suddenly stops working and you can't access the final version of your slides.
Write about:
  • what went wrong;
  • how a pessimist might react;
  • how an optimist might react;
  • how a realist might react;
  • what you could do to solve the problem;
  • how you could turn the situation into an opportunity.
Useful language
Something went wrong when...
A pessimist might think...
An optimist might say...
A realist would probably...
Instead of focusing on...
I could deal with the situation by...
I could turn the problem into an opportunity by...
The important thing is to keep a positive attitude...
Model text

I've been preparing an important presentation for several days, but on the morning of the presentation, my computer suddenly stops working. A pessimist might think that the whole presentation is going to be a disaster. An optimist might hope that everything will work out somehow.

A realist would probably focus on finding a practical solution. I could try to access my files from another device or contact someone who can help me. If the slides are unavailable, I could still give a simpler presentation using my notes.

I could also turn the problem into an opportunity to become more confident speaking without slides. After that, I would start keeping backup copies of important files.

Expression Patterns · Might / Could / Would probably
📌 Expression Patterns
PatternExample
focus on + nounLet's focus on the main problem.
deal with + nounWe need to deal with reality.
turn something into something elseWe can turn this problem into an opportunity.
something goes / went wrongSomething went wrong during the presentation.
hope for the best / expect the worstHope for the best, but expect the worst.
keep a positive attitudeShe managed to keep a positive attitude.
💡 Tip: After focus on and deal with, use a noun: focus on the solution, deal with the problem. With turn, remember into: turn a problem into an opportunity.
🔮 Talking About Possible Reactions
StructureUseExample
might + verba possible reactionA pessimist might think it's a disaster.
could + verba possible action or solutionI could use another computer.
would probably + verbthe reaction we expectA realist would probably look for a solution.
💡 Tip: After might, could and would, use the base form of the verb: A pessimist might think… (not thinks or to think).
