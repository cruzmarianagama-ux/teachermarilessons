Three Cooking Classes · After the Cooking Class
Exercise 1 – Three Cooking Classes
Read the descriptions and choose the correct class for each person.

CLASS A — COOK WITH A CHEF

Learn professional techniques while preparing a three-course dinner. The chef demonstrates each technique first, and students then prepare the dishes themselves. Some cooking experience is recommended.


CLASS B — EASY COOKING

Never cooked before? No problem. Learn how to prepare four simple meals using inexpensive ingredients and basic kitchen equipment. Perfect for beginners.


CLASS C — CREATIVE KITCHEN

There is no fixed recipe in this class. Students receive a selection of ingredients and work in small groups to create an original dish. At the end, everyone tastes the dishes.

📌 Talking About Experiences: Have you ever...?
FormExample
Have / Has + subject + ever + past participle?Have you ever baked bread?
Has Leo ever grilled meat?
Short answersYes, I have. / No, I haven't.
Yes, she has. / No, she hasn't.
I / You / We / They + have ('ve)I've made pizza several times.
He / She / It + has ('s)She's never taken a cooking class.
💡 Tip: Use have with I, you, we, they and has with he, she, it. Answer a Have you ever...? question with have / haven't, not did or do.
⏱️ Experience or Specific Event?
Present perfectPast simple
Experience in your life. No specific time.A finished event. We say or ask when.
I've made pizza before.I made pizza last Friday.
Have you ever taken a cooking class?When did you take the class? Last summer.
💡 Tip: Time words like yesterday, last weekend, on Friday, in 2020 go with the past simple: I made it yesterday (not I have made it yesterday).

⭐ Ambitions and Dreams
StructureExample
I'd like to + verbI'd like to open a small café one day.
I'd love to + verbI'd love to take part in a cooking show.
I'd prefer to + verbI'd prefer to cook in my clients' kitchens.
My dream is to + verbMy dream is to become a personal chef.
💡 Giving Advice
StructureExample
You should + verbYou should learn about costs first.
You shouldn't + verbYou shouldn't open a restaurant tomorrow.
You could + verb (a softer idea)You could practise cooking under pressure.
💡 Tip: After should / could, use the verb with no to: You should learn (not You should to learn).
