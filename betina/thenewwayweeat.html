Exercise 3 – Writing Challenge
Imagine you are planning to start a delivery-only food business. Write 120–160 words describing your business and the type of kitchen you would need.
Include in your text:
  • The type of food you would sell
  • Whether you would open your own kitchen or rent a ready-to-use kitchen
  • At least two pieces of kitchen equipment you would need
  • One important requirement about location, capacity, storage or opening hours
  • One possible running cost
  • One possible problem related to food quality, delivery drivers or customer loyalty
  • One thing you would try to negotiate before signing a contract
  • At least two negotiation/business expressions from the unit
Useful language:
I'd like to set up...  |  I would choose to...  |  A fully equipped kitchen would...
One of the main advantages is...  |  One possible problem is...
Our main concern would be...  |  We would need...

Model text
I'd like to set up a delivery-only business selling fresh pasta and Italian meals. I would choose to rent a ready-to-use kitchen because the upfront costs would be lower and I could start operating quickly.

The kitchen would need to be fully equipped with ovens, refrigerators and large prep tables. I would also need enough storage for fresh ingredients and packaging. A location near the city centre would be important because long delivery times could affect food quality.

One of my main running costs would be ingredients, so I would need to control food costs carefully. Customer loyalty could also be challenging because customers wouldn't visit a dining space.

Before signing the contract, my main concern would be the monthly rent. I would ask, "Would you be willing to reduce the rent?" If the owner could offer a better price and include maintenance, I'd be prepared to sign a 12-month contract.

Negotiation expressions and business language from this unit.
Polite Negotiation Requests
ExpressionUse
Would you be willing to reduce the rent?Asking politely for a change
Could you include another freezer?Requesting something extra
We'd be interested in renting the kitchen, but...Showing interest + adding a condition
Our main concern is the storage space.Explaining your priority
We would need more storage space.Stating a requirement
These expressions are polite. Use them to ask for changes without sounding rude or aggressive.
Conditional Offers
StructureExample
If you could + base verb...If you could reduce the rent,
...we'd be prepared to + base verb...we'd be prepared to sign today.
That would make the offer...That would make the offer more attractive to us.
Pattern: If you could [do X], we'd be prepared to [do Y]. — Both sides give something. This makes the negotiation fair.
