What's Your Roommate Like? · Multiple Choice
Exercise 1 – What's Your Roommate Like?
Listen to the conversation and choose the correct answer.
Transcript:
MOM: Hi, sweetie! How's college going? Have you settled into your new apartment yet?
DAUGHTER: Hey, Mom! Yeah, it's going pretty well. It felt a little strange at first, but now I'm getting used to it. My roommates are all really nice, so that makes a big difference.
MOM: I'm glad to hear that! I was a bit nervous about you living with people you didn't know. So, tell me about them! What are your roommates like?
DAUGHTER: There are three of them—Emily, Mark, and Lily. We all get along pretty well. Emily was the first person I met. She's tall and slim, with long, straight brown hair. She usually wears colorful sweatshirts and sneakers, and she's very cheerful—always smiling. She's super friendly and funny; she tells jokes all the time. She's also outgoing and likes planning movie nights or group dinners for us. We get along really well.
MOM: She sounds like a great person to live with! What about Mark?
DAUGHTER: Mark's really different. He's a bit shorter than Emily and quite skinny, with short, curly blond hair. He wears glasses and has blue eyes. He seemed a little shy when we first met, but once you talk to him, he's really kind and helpful. He actually loves cooking, so he's always asking if anyone wants some food or snacks. Last weekend, he made homemade pizza for everyone. He's also very responsible with his homework. Oh, and he's very reliable—when he says he'll do something, you know he will.
MOM: Wow, that's awesome! He sounds great—I already love him. And what about Lily?
DAUGHTER: Lily's pretty quiet and a little more serious. She's average height, with dark, curly hair, and she usually wears jeans and hoodies. She's super smart and hardworking—she studies a lot. So, she spends a lot of time in her room, but she's also friendly when you get to know her. She helped me with my math homework yesterday and explained everything really well. It's great to have someone I can go to with my math questions.
MOM: It sounds like you've got a good mix of people! Do you all hang out together?
DAUGHTER: Yeah. We try to. Sometimes, we eat dinner together or watch TV in the living room. Emily loves planning stuff—she wants us to go to a soccer game next weekend. Mark's always up for board games, and Lily likes to join us for movie nights even though she's usually busy with her books.
MOM: I'm so happy you found good roommates. It sounds like you all get along well and help each other out.
DAUGHTER: Yeah, I feel lucky. I was worried I wouldn't fit in, but everyone's friendly and easy to talk to. We all have different personalities, but it works. I think this year's going to be really good.
MOM: That's great, honey. Remember, if you ever need anything or just want to talk, I'm always here.
DAUGHTER: I know, Mom. Thanks. I'll call you soon!
MOM: Okay, take care, sweetheart. Love you!
DAUGHTER: Love you too! Bye, Mom.

Find the Perfect Roommate
Writing Task – Find the Perfect Roommate
Read the situation and the profiles below, then write a short paragraph.

You are moving into a shared apartment. Read the profiles of the three roommates and decide who is the best roommate for you.
Roommate A – Emily
• 20 years old
• Studies Marketing
• Very outgoing and friendly
• Loves sports and movie nights
• Enjoys meeting new people
• Often organizes group activities
Roommate B – Mark
• 21 years old
• Studies Engineering
• Quiet at first, but very kind
• Loves cooking
• Responsible and reliable
• Enjoys board games
Roommate C – Lily
• 20 years old
• Studies Mathematics
• Smart and hardworking
• Quiet and serious
• Loves reading
• Often helps friends with schoolwork
Task: Write a paragraph (60–80 words) and use these questions as a guide:
• Which roommate would you choose?
• Why?
• What do you have in common?
• What activities would you do together?
