Grammar Bank
Going to — affirmative, negative, questions
Affirmative — I / you / we / they / he / she / it
SubjectStructureExample
II'm going to + verbI'm going to take the bus.
He / She / ItHe's / She's going to + verbShe's going to stay at the hotel.
You / We / TheyWe're going to + verbThey're going to miss the flight.
We use going to for plans and intentions we already have decided.
Negative
SubjectStructureExample
II'm not going to + verbI'm not going to take a taxi.
He / She / ItHe isn't going to + verbHe isn't going to print the boarding pass.
You / We / TheyWe aren't going to + verbWe aren't going to stay long.
Yes / No Questions
QuestionShort Answer
Are you going to take a taxi?Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
Is she going to fly to Rome?Yes, she is. / No, she isn't.
Are they going to stay here?Yes, they are. / No, they aren't.
WH- Questions
Question wordExample
WhereWhere are you going to stay?
WhenWhen are they going to travel?
HowHow are you going to get to the airport?
How longHow long are you going to stay?

WhatWhat are you going to do after the flight?
Common mistake: "Where you are going to stay?" is wrong. The verb are comes before the subject: "Where are you going to stay?"
