When and how to use quick responses
💬 What Are Quick Responses?

Quick responses are short fixed phrases we use immediately when someone gives us news, makes a suggestion, or needs help. They show that we are listening and reacting naturally.

SituationNatural response(s)
You hear surprising newsNo way! / You're kidding! / You don't say! / That can't be true!
Someone has good newsCongratulations! / No way! (positive surprise)
Someone has bad newsThat's too bad. / What a pity! / I'm sorry to hear that.
Someone is nervous / worriedDon't worry! / Good luck!
Someone is going somewhere funHave fun!
Someone suggests a planSounds good! / Sure, why not?
Someone asks for permissionSure, no problem! / It's OK with me!
An arrangement is confirmedFine, see you then!
Someone thanks youYou're welcome! / No problem!
Someone sneezesBless you!
Someone is very slowHurry up!
⚠️ Common Mistakes
WrongRightWhy
"What a pity!" (for good news)Congratulations!What a pity expresses sadness, not celebration.
"Have fun!" (for bad news)I'm sorry to hear that.Have fun is only for positive situations.
"Sounds good!" (to refuse)I'm sorry, I can't.Sounds good always accepts, never refuses.
"Bless you!" (to thank someone)You're welcome!Bless you is only after a sneeze.
💡 Tip: Before you react, think: is this good news, bad news, surprising news, a suggestion, or a request? Match your response to the situation, not just the words.
📌 Tone Matters

The same expression can mean different things depending on context and tone:

ExpressionContext AContext B
No way!Surprise: "She won €20,000." → "No way!"Refusal: "Can I borrow your laptop for a month?" → "No way!"
No problem!You're welcome: "Thanks for helping." → "No problem!"Accepting: "Can I use your phone?" → "Sure, no problem!"
