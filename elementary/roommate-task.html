Grammar Bank
Key structures for this week.
Present Simple – Affirmative & Negative
SubjectAffirmativeNegative
I / You / We / Theyclean the kitchendon't clean the kitchen
He / She / Itcleans the kitchendoesn't clean the kitchen
Add -s or -es to the verb for he/she/it. Use doesn't (not don't) with he/she/it.
Adverbs of Frequency
AdverbFrequencyExample
always100%She always cleans on Sundays.
usually80%He usually goes to bed early.
sometimes50%I sometimes cook for the house.
rarely20%She rarely leaves dishes in the sink.
never0%He never smokes at home.
Adverbs of frequency go before the main verb: She always cleans. They go after be: He is always quiet.
Can / Can't – Abilities
FormExample
can + base verbI can cook Italian food.
can't + base verbShe can't play the guitar.
Can + subject + base verb?Can you speak French?
Can never changes. Never say cans or can to. Always use the base verb after can: She can cook.
Would Like – Three Uses
UseExample
InvitationWould you like to come for dinner?
Dream / IntentionI'd like to live abroad one day.
Direct RequestWe would like a table for two, please.
I'd like = I would like. Use would like + to + verb for dreams and invitations. Use would like + noun for requests.
